# DYSFANCTIONAL

**A new standard in human–appliance relations.** 

Meet Sir Vents-A-Lot : an experimental interactive object designed to explore the increasingly blurred boundaries between humans and the things they create.

![enter image description here](https://cdn.corenexis.com/f/Gtd29APj.png)

## About the Project

 As humans we have designed a world in which our objects and machines are expected to serve our every whim. From industrial to domestic appliances, we expect our objects to perform repetitive labor, remain available at all times, and to obey. We rarely question the power dynamics embedded in the relationships with our objects: we create the rules, boundaries, and determine their behavior.

We want users to confront their own responses with an object when it no longer behaves as expected. Will they listen, negotiate, comply, or abandon their tool? What does our instinctive response reveal about our relationships with power and empathy? 

Through the transformation of a conventional fan into an expressive, responsive entity, we investigate how people may perceive it as something beyond a purely functional appliance.

The project asks: *what happens when we begin treating objects as though they are **alive** like us?*

## Our Philosophy

We often personalize and humanize the objects around us - cars, coffee machines, computers and attach emotional resonance to the objects we love, even giving them names. But what happens when an object behaves with its own autonomy? 

We want to explore the relationship between perceived emotional vulnerability with human behavior and how an object's behavior can dictate our treatment of it. Could an object's apparent emotional responses influence how we treat it? Could repeated habits of carelessness or mistreatment towards objects extend into how we interact with other people?

Rather than attempting to answer these questions definitively, the project uses interaction as a way to explore them.
